02 The Blind Spot of Single-Request Classifiers

Prior to EFS, enterprises with strict privacy mandates relied on Legacy Zero Data Retention (ZDR). Under legacy ZDR, an AI vendor processes an API prompt in memory, runs real-time inline safety classifiers on that single request/response pair, and immediately discards the payload without writing logs to disk. While legacy ZDR protected privacy, Anthropic's operational threat intelligence proved it was blind to modern autonomous attacks. In September 2026, Anthropic documented a real-world campaign targeting roughly 50 organizations in which an AI agent divided a cyber intrusion across many parallel sub-agents, saving harvested credentials between sessions to resume operations seamlessly. Viewed one request at a time, every isolated prompt looked harmless—allowing the campaign to evade single-turn classifiers completely.

03 The June 2026 Backlash: Security vs. Custody

To catch multi-turn, cross-session campaigns, Anthropic initially announced in June 2026 that frontier models (Mythos, Fable, and successors) would require 30-day server-side log retention. Although Anthropic pledged never to train on the data, regulated buyers rejected vendor-hosted retention outright. As Emergent News noted, internal evaluations confirmed that forcing regulated banks and defense contractors to waive ZDR created severe commercial friction against competing providers. 04 How Split-Custody Detection Works

  • ▪Bring Your Own Storage (BYOS) & Keys: Enterprises provision an Amazon S3 bucket or Google Cloud Storage bucket inside their own cloud account, protected by their own Customer-Managed Encryption Keys (CMEK) and IAM audit policies.
  • ▪Prompt Deletion & Cloud Boundaries: Inputs and outputs are deleted promptly from Anthropic/cloud inference pipelines once written to customer storage. When deployed via Amazon Bedrock or Google Cloud (Google Managed), the entire data flow stays strictly inside that cloud boundary.
  • ▪15-Minute Automated Scans & Metadata-Only Safety Cases: Every 15 minutes, automated safety monitoring analyzes patterns across requests inside the customer's enclave. When a risk is flagged—such as an agent using a software exploit to probe an internal database or orchestrating parallel offensive cyber tasks—EFS generates a Safety Case. Both the customer and Anthropic receive the alert notification via API or storage, but Safety Cases exclude raw input and output transcripts.
