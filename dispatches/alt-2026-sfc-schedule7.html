01 The Compliance Scope Dilemma: The Algorithmic Trading Misconception

When licensed asset managers (SFC Type 9) and advisory firms (SFC Type 4) deploy artificial intelligence to synthesize research, summarize corporate filings, or calculate DCF valuations, internal compliance committees frequently experience severe scope paralysis. External legal advisors and risk officers frequently confound 'algorithmic trading' with 'algorithmic reasoning', threatening to subject internal research copilots to the onerous requirements of Schedule 7 to the SFC Code of Conduct.

Schedule 7 mandates exhaustive pre-trade risk controls, automated kill switches, ultra-low-latency real-time market risk monitors, and annual direct market access certifications. Applying these requirements to an internal research summarizer creates immense compliance friction—delaying deployment by 6 to 9 months and adding hundreds of thousands of dollars in unnecessary consulting fees.

The solution requires establishing an airtight, mathematically provable demarcation boundary between internal decision support systems and regulated algorithmic trading facilities.

02 Statutory Analysis of SFC Code of Conduct Schedule 7 & DMA Requirements

Under paragraph 1.1 of Schedule 7, 'Algorithmic Trading' is defined strictly as: 'computer trading where each order is generated automatically based on a predefined mathematical model or algorithm, without human intervention in determining any of the trading parameters (including price, volume, timing, or whether to submit the order)'.
