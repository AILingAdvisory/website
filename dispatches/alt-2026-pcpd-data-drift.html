01 The Day-2 Compliance Drift Trap: How Financial AI Silently Breaches PDPO

A systemic vulnerability across Hong Kong financial institutions is the divergence between initial AI deployment approvals and subsequent operational realities—a phenomenon termed 'Day-2 Compliance Drift'.

On Day 1, an enterprise AI research copilot is approved by the Data Protection Officer (DPO) and Legal Counsel based on a pristine, synthetic dataset containing only public HKEX disclosures and anonymized market commentary.

On Day 2, internal engineering squads seeking to enhance utility connect the copilot to internal client CRM repositories, communication archives, or wealth onboarding forms. Instantly, the model begins ingesting Personal Identifiable Information (PII)—including client names, HKID numbers, net worth statements, and investment risk profiles—triggering silent, catastrophic compliance drift under Hong Kong PDPO Cap. 486.

02 PCPD Model Personal Data Protection Framework: The 4 Core Pillars

The Office of the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data (PCPD) established the Model Personal Data Protection Framework for Artificial Intelligence to guide organisations in the ethical and compliant adoption of generative systems. The framework rests on four operational pillars:

Pillar 1: Strategy & Governance

AI Governance Committee charter, executive allocation, and data classification policies.

Pillar 2: Risk Assessment & HITL

Mandatory Privacy Impact Assessments (PIA) and defined human oversight intervention levels.

Pillar 3: Lifecycle Data Protection

Strict data minimization, PII tokenisation, avoiding unvetted model re-training.

Pillar 4: Transparency & Recourse

Updated PICS declarations, AI disclosure labels, and statutory data subject rights fulfillment.

03 Operationalising Data Protection Principles (DPPs 1–6) in LLM Architectures

Financial institutions must translate the six Data Protection Principles (DPPs) of Schedule 1 to the PDPO into technical runtime constraints:

  • DPP 1 (Purpose & Manner): Enforcing input-side filtering to reject unneeded personal attributes before LLM ingestion.
  • DPP 2 (Accuracy & Retention): Automated TTL (time-to-live) expiration and synchronization on vector retrieval indexes.
  • DPP 3 (Use Limitation): Cryptographic purpose limitation blocking cross-departmental data contamination.
  • DPP 4 (Data Security): Field-level encryption for vector embeddings and ephemeral prompt cache deletion.
  • DPP 5 & 6 (Openness & Access): Standardized PICS disclosure and deterministic mechanisms to excise data from retrieval indexes.
